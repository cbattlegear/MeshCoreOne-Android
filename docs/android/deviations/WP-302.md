### Durable notification handoff dependency

The WP-401 owner reports that the published #515 head
`2c6423600087a4c22c7ce96c679a206edb2501ca` has no durable staging or
acknowledgement API. Its uncommitted draft deletes the pending record on
acknowledgement and is not suitable for integration: replaying the same post
could create another delivery after the record is deleted. A future producer
must preserve the consumed post-to-delivery identity atomically without
retaining its payload, keep pending capacity separate, and resolve retention
and legacy-token policy. The owner has not supplied a frozen consumable
contract or actual reservation receipt. A proposed replacement would retain
the post-to-delivery identity and sequence after acknowledgement, but its
indefinite-retention and legacy-token policies are not approved.

The provisional producer identity is the complete tuple of store identity,
post ID, delivery ID and producer sequence. The WP-401 owner agrees that the
accepted row must also retain the shared ingress position in the same producer
transaction: a producer-only sequence cannot order a tap against a Back or
lifecycle action durably recorded in another store. Duplicate staging requires
consistent identity and payload; acknowledgement compares the complete
identity. Missing, mismatched, corrupt, unavailable and indeterminate results
remain distinct. This is a proposed port, not behavior supplied by the held
AtomicFile draft.

The WP-401 owner proposes a bounded, paginated inventory of both pending and
consumed identities with a coherent store revision or high-water bound and an
explicit completion marker. Continuations must preserve that view or report
invalidation; live offset pagination is not a completeness proof. A pending
scan plus `Inspect` of known identities cannot discover a consumed delivery
whose receipt is missing. No consumed identities may be excluded by age
without an approved evidence-preserving compaction or equivalent completeness
proof. Cross-store recovery also needs a shared-ingress startup fence or
revision revalidation; even a coherent producer snapshot alone can be stale by
the time the consumer journal is read.

After acknowledgement removes the payload, a tombstone containing identity
alone cannot detect a duplicate post with changed payload. Versioned canonical
comparison evidence or authenticated immutable issuance are possible designs,
but neither is selected. An ordinary digest is not authentication or proof
of payload erasure and needs privacy, retention and key-lifetime review as
applicable.

WP-302 must durably commit and replay the resolved destination and the
navigation state required to preserve existing selection and back stacks
before asking the producer to acknowledge the complete delivery identity; an
in-memory navigation CAS or an Activity saved-state Bundle does not establish
that guarantee. A prepared receipt alone cannot authorize acknowledgement;
after restart its original delivery must remain pending. A receipt later
superseded by Back, tab selection or a route-removing lifecycle change must
remain recognizable so an acknowledgement retry cannot reopen it. A newer
route can leave an earlier destination in a back stack: changing the active
route is not by itself proof that the earlier destination was removed. The
current independent navigation CAS and synchronous user actions do not provide
a durable supersession order, and manager transitions during disk I/O need a
crash-safe ownership witness. No journal protocol is approved, and neither the
producer nor consumer durable handoff is integrated or verified here.

#### Non-activated durable-transition design

The proposed integration uses one ordered ingress for producer OPEN staging,
navigation intents and route-affecting lifecycle transitions. A startup fence
must establish the authoritative ordered pending deliveries before accepting
new user-navigation commits; otherwise a Back received while an older tap
has not yet been indexed cannot be ordered against that tap. Producer sequence
alone does not order independent Back/tab events. The WP-401 owner proposes
reserving FIFO ingress tickets before suspension and performing stage and
journal transactions in the same process-owned lane. Startup reconciles
pending identities and receipts before releasing buffered navigation tickets;
it must not hold that lane while awaiting radio READY or asynchronous lookup.
The current producer does not provide this lane. Source manifests inspected
here do not declare a separate process for the current OPEN receiver or main
activity, but a merged-manifest/runtime single-process guarantee is unverified;
multiple staging processes would require a durable cross-process ordering
mechanism. The existing legacy OPEN queue remains unchanged until producer,
consumer and dispatcher are activated together; the WP-215 action receipt is
not a durable OPEN receipt.

An actual manager transition would mint a unique ticket under its lifecycle
boundary, bound to the process epoch, lifecycle revision, actual session/token
and relevant adoption or retirement identity. Revocation may happen
immediately, but a route-affecting successor publication must not pass the
ordered lane before its transition and any required supersession have been
durably recorded. Journal I/O and observable publication remain outside the
manager, registry, graph and app locks. A same-radio rebuild that retains its
current selection and back stack is not automatically a supersession: an
unsettled route must be revalidated against the replacement session without
reopening it as a new tap. An actual radio change, user navigation or other
route-removing transition requires an explicit ordered outcome. Which
lifecycle events retain or supersede a route must follow the actual runtime
and navigation contract, not be inferred from revocation alone.

For a tap, the lane retains the producer's complete delivery identity and
ingress position, captures the owner before lookup, and persists `Prepared`
without acknowledging it. A manager-guarded CAS can produce a deferred
publication; only a durable resolved/restorable destination or explicit
terminal outcome, ordered against the manager ticket and later supersessions,
is a candidate acknowledgement receipt. The destination contains stable
radio/entity/message identifiers and other required routing metadata, not
message content or a DTO. The producer
acknowledgement itself runs outside lifecycle locks and must compare the
complete identity and be idempotent. A failed or result-lost acknowledgement
keeps the same receipt and retries the same identity, never a new navigation.
Producer persistence and retention semantics remain proposed, not frozen.

Back, tab selection and other route-changing entry points need synchronous
ingress tickets tied to the revision and state actually observed by the UI,
before waiting for disk or the lane. Reading the latest published
`StateFlow.value` is not proof that the user saw it. Otherwise a Back queued
behind an unpublished or not-yet-rendered tap can pop a destination the user
never saw. A cancelled predictive-Back gesture alone must not commit Back or
supersede a delivery. Ordered user-intent projections must also preserve
successive Back actions and per-tab stacks. The lane records a
superseding action and its replayable outcome before publishing that action;
a failed or indeterminate write must not be reported or acknowledged as a
durable outcome, and an ambiguous commit must be reread. The recovery and
interaction UX remain unresolved; this does not choose a blanket UI-blocking
policy. The current `NavigationStateStore` has a logical
revision and deferred publication. A local, unexecuted candidate now exposes a
separate published snapshot flow, retaining the revision even for equal-valued
states and withholding deferred commits until publication. Its authored tests
cover those distinctions. It does not prove that a snapshot was rendered to
the user, supply guarded rollback/rebase of a hidden CAS, or install an ingress
lane. Current `NavigationCoordinator` and mounted shell actions remain unchanged
by this primitive.
The existing WP-302 reservation at revision 29 covers the store and related
`NavigationStateTest.kt` through `wp-owned/modify` and
`reviewed-support-test/add-or-update-related-test`, respectively. Source
validation with pinned `C:\Python312\python.exe` reported the manifest valid
and 2,008 provenance records; these checks did not compile or execute the
three newly authored Kotlin tests.

On restart, `Prepared` leaves the original producer delivery pending and is
retried with the same identity after fresh readiness and lookup. A committed
route is revalidated against the new actual session, radio and entity before
restoration or acknowledgement; the old process/session stamp is not new
session authority. A durably superseded route is not restored, and its
recorded user/terminal outcome must be applied or replayed before settlement.
Ambiguous storage completion is resolved by rereading the durable state.
`Pending` with no matching receipt can be treated as new work only inside a
positively initialized, consistent journal. `Consumed` with only `Prepared`,
or with a missing, corrupt or mismatched receipt, is an inconsistency unless a
separately approved compaction protocol retains equivalent evidence.
Missing readiness, a mismatched radio, unknown identity, corrupt storage or
an unproved manager witness is an explicit blocked outcome, not success.
The product's private back-stack restoration and the bounds/retention policy
still need an approved design; Activity saved state is not a substitute. In
